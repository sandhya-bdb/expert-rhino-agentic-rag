Assam Government Official Position on Kaziranga Eco-Sensitive Zone (ESZ)
Key Figure: Chief Minister Himanta Biswa Sarma & Cabinet Minister Pijush Hazarika

OFFICIAL ARGUMENTS & STANCE:
1. Livelihood and Development: The Assam government maintains that the default 10-km ESZ buffer around Kaziranga National Park is too restrictive and halts all economic, infrastructural, and civic development in surrounding settlements like Bokakhat, Kaliabor, and Jakhalabandha.
2. Rationalization to 1-km Minimum: The state cabinet approved a proposal to formally notify the ESZ boundary at a minimum of 1 km and up to 3 km in specific places, utilizing the Supreme Court's April 2023 ruling allowing site-specific proposals.
3. Civic Relief: The government argues that by shrinking the buffer zone, local residents will no longer face stringent environmental clearance hurdles for building houses, schools, public roads, and running local businesses.
4. Core Park Protection: Officials emphasize that this rationalization applies only to the external buffer zone (ESZ) and has absolutely no impact on the core area of Kaziranga National Park itself, which remains under maximum wildlife protection status.

CONSERVATIONIST & ACTIVIST OPPOSITION:
1. Corridor Fragmentation: Environmentalists argue that wild animals (elephants, rhinos, tigers) do not recognize administrative borders. They rely on critical "wildlife corridors" to escape annual monsoonal floods by migrating to the higher ground of the Karbi Anglong hills. A 1-km buffer will lead to commercial developments (resorts, hotels) that block these corridors.
2. Encroachment & Real Estate: Activists accuse the government of pushing the 1-km limit to benefit luxury hotel chains, mining operators, and commercial developers who want to buy land right next to the park's boundaries.
3. Arrest of Activists: Tensions have escalated with local communities protesting land acquisition for resorts. Activist Pranab Doley and others have been detained under strict laws (like the National Security Act) for leading protests against local displacements in areas near Kaziranga (like Ingle Pathar and Hatikhuli Bagicha).
