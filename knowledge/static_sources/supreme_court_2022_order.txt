Supreme Court of India Order (June 3, 2022) - Eco-Sensitive Zones (ESZ)
Case: In Re: T.N. Godavarman Thirumulpad vs. Union of India & Ors.

RULING & DIRECTIVES:
1. Mandatory 1-km Buffer Zone: The Supreme Court directed that every national park and wildlife sanctuary in the country must have a mandatory Eco-Sensitive Zone (ESZ) of a minimum of one kilometer, measured from the demarcated boundary of such protected area.
2. Restriction on Activities: Mining within the designated ESZ of national parks and wildlife sanctuaries is strictly prohibited. No permanent structures shall be built for any purpose within the ESZ.
3. Ongoing Projects: Any ongoing developmental activities or permanent structures within the 1-km ESZ must seek permission from the Chief Conservator of Forests and are subject to strict monitoring.
4. Default 10-km Rule: In cases where the buffer zone is not officially notified or is pending notification, a default 10-km radius around the protected area shall be treated as the Eco-Sensitive Zone until the formal notification is complete.

IMPLICATION FOR KAZIRANGA:
For Kaziranga National Park and Tiger Reserve, in the absence of a site-specific final notification, this order initially meant that a default 10-km buffer zone was applied, severely restricting construction, commercial activities, and infrastructure development in the surrounding Kaliabor, Bokakhat, and Jakhalabandha areas.
